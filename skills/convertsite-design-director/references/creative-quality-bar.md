# Creative quality bar

Passing component, accessibility, and conversion checks is necessary but not sufficient. Deliver a site only when it also feels authored, coherent, and worth shipping.

## What “wow” means

Do not equate wow with maximalism, animation, or decoration. Create one strong, relevant idea that makes the experience memorable while keeping behavior obvious.

Examples include a distinctive spatial composition, brand-specific image treatment, an ownable graphic device, an unusual but readable content rhythm, or a visual metaphor connected to the offer.

Large typography, a bright accent color, and a full-width stock photo do not create a signature by themselves.

## Concept selection in explore mode

Use this file only when the user explicitly activates `Creative mode: explore`. Consider the three concepts produced by `concept-gate.md`. Select the concept that best combines:

- Brand and category fit
- Conversion clarity
- Strength of available assets
- Visual memorability
- Feasibility in ConvertSite

Do not average the concepts together.

## Ship bar

Require all of the following:

- The first viewport communicates category, value, and primary action within five seconds.
- The site has one recognizable signature idea beyond typography and palette.
- At least one composition feels specifically designed rather than selected from a standard landing-page pattern.
- Section rhythm varies intentionally; do not repeat the same composition more than twice consecutively.
- Imagery has a coherent subject, crop, lighting, and color direction.
- The visual vocabulary is visibly grounded in the real subject rather than a fashionable generic style cluster.
- Copy is specific to the category and audience without inventing business facts.
- The primary conversion can actually be completed.
- Mobile retains both the signature idea and conversion hierarchy.
- The page contains no obvious filler section.

## First-render rejection test

After rendering, ask:

1. Would this still look plausible after replacing the logo and industry nouns?
2. Is the memorable quality only a large headline, accent color, or hero photo?
3. Does the page rely on repeated equal columns or cards?
4. Does the primary CTA end without a completion mechanism?
5. Would a strong designer reasonably ship this without another visual pass?

If any of the first four answers is yes, or the fifth is no, perform at most one focused revision before delivery. Fix the underlying concept, composition, asset direction, rhythm, or endpoint rather than adding decoration.

As the final creative check, remove one non-functional flourish. Keep it removed when hierarchy, meaning, and memorability do not suffer.
