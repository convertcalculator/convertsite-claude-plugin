# Design-direction library

These are systems, not templates. Select one primary direction. A compatible secondary influence may contribute at most 20%; never average several directions together.

## Selection method

Evaluate:

- Desired brand character
- Audience expectations and risk sensitivity
- Emotional versus rational buying motivation
- Amount and quality of imagery
- Content density
- Importance of expertise, proof, craft, or aspiration
- Existing brand assets and styles

Reject directions that depend on assets the project does not have. Prefer the direction that best expresses the offer while keeping the conversion contract intact.

### Standard creative decision gate

Before any MCP write, make the creative choice explicit. This is a short internal comparison, not three developed concepts and not a reason to delay a well-scoped build.

1. Name the central creative tension in this brief, such as institutional authority versus personal intimacy, technical evidence versus life aspiration, quiet reassurance versus active momentum, or photography-led storytelling versus diagram-led explanation.
2. State two or three plausible positions that would each suit the audience and offer. Describe the strategic idea, not a palette or a list of components.
3. Select one position and explain in one sentence why it best supports the primary conversion.
4. Reject at least one credible alternative and record why it is weaker for this brief. A direction is not deliberate if no plausible alternative was considered.
5. Translate the selected position into one primary library direction, an optional secondary influence capped at 20%, one signature motif, an image role, an informational-visual role, a text-density strategy, and an interaction posture.

Do not expose this comparison to the user unless they ask to participate in the choice. Do not ask a preference question when the brief contains enough evidence to decide. When the user has explicitly supplied market examples, use them to identify contrasting positions and category conventions; do not copy, blend, or rank the sites as templates. Common category styling is evidence of a convention, not proof that it is the right direction.

Create a visual-communication plan, not only a styling plan. For each major section, choose the clearest carrier for the idea: concise copy, photography, illustration, meaningful iconography, diagram, comparison, verified data, interface preview, or interaction. Distinguish atmospheric assets, which create tone, from informational visuals, which improve comprehension. Do not use prose because it is easiest to generate. Do not add generic icons merely to decorate headings.

For a multi-section site, avoid letting one hero image carry the entire visual identity while every later section becomes text on a surface. A second unrelated mood photograph adds atmosphere but does not satisfy an informational-visual need. When content presents three or more categories, steps, choices, comparisons, or future tools, evaluate and normally use a scannable system such as distinct category symbols, a process diagram, annotated pathway, comparison treatment, or product preview. When assets are limited, keep these devices restrained and coherent. Never invent evidence, metrics, or client material to fill this role.

Strongly consider **Immersive Full-Screen Photography** when the location, landscape, architecture, destination, or physical environment is central to the purchase and strong wide-format imagery is available. Do not require the user to explicitly request “full-screen” when these conditions clearly make it the strongest direction.

## 1. Architectural Editorial

Best for architecture, interiors, premium services, property, and considered purchases.

- Composition: offset grids, strong alignment, varied image scale
- Type: expressive editorial display with restrained functional text
- Imagery: deliberate crops, material details, case-study sequences
- Surfaces: restrained, fine rules, limited card containers
- Rhythm: spacious with occasional dense technical moments
- Signature: captions, coordinates, plan-like annotations, or numbered details
- Avoid: generic icon grids, excessive pills, equal-sized repeated cards

## 2. Cinematic Impact

Best for aspirational offers with exceptional photography or video.

- Composition: immersive hero, layered text, decisive scale changes
- Type: large, concise headlines; minimal supporting copy
- Imagery: full-bleed, atmospheric, controlled overlay and grading
- Surfaces: dark or high-contrast fields with quiet controls
- Rhythm: immersive opening followed by calmer evidence sections
- Signature: a recurring frame, crop, light treatment, or chapter transition
- Avoid: weak source imagery, dense text over images, effects before clarity

This direction may use framed or contained photography. Do not select it when the brief specifically calls for photography to occupy the full viewport; use **Immersive Full-Screen Photography** instead.

## 3. Premium Restraint

Best for high-trust, high-value brands that should feel calm and assured.

- Composition: disciplined grid, generous margins, precise alignment
- Type: refined scale contrast without oversized novelty
- Imagery: few excellent images rather than many average ones, supported where useful by purposeful detail crops, diagrams, artifacts, or restrained symbols
- Surfaces: tonal, sharp or subtly rounded, almost no shadow
- Rhythm: quiet and deliberate
- Signature: one material color, fine border system, or restrained monogram
- Avoid: decorative abundance, loud gradients, dense badge collections, or a single hero image followed by a text-only page disguised as restraint

## 4. Warm Craft

Best for makers, construction, food, hospitality, local services, and tangible quality.

- Composition: image-led storytelling mixed with practical information
- Type: warm display face with highly readable supporting text
- Imagery: hands, materials, process, lived-in environments
- Surfaces: tactile colors, natural contrast, grounded shapes
- Rhythm: human stories alternating with concrete proof
- Signature: material swatches, process marks, stamps, or crafted dividers
- Avoid: artificial rustic styling, generic sustainability symbolism

## 5. Technical Confidence

Best for complex products, engineering, finance, energy, B2B, and evidence-led decisions.

- Composition: structured grids, comparisons, diagrams, clear data hierarchy
- Type: precise and compact with strong numeric treatment
- Imagery: product, system, diagrams, real implementation evidence
- Surfaces: crisp borders, tables, restrained information panels
- Rhythm: compact proof balanced with explanatory breathing room
- Signature: live metrics, specifications, or evidence annotations
- Avoid: dashboard overload, unexplained jargon, decorative data

## 6. Bold Contemporary

Best for challengers, cultural brands, creative services, and younger audiences.

- Composition: strong blocks, surprising but legible alignment, scale contrast
- Type: confident display typography with simple body text
- Imagery: graphic crops, cutouts, art-directed color treatment
- Surfaces: flat color, visible borders, minimal shadow
- Rhythm: energetic alternation without constant intensity
- Signature: oversized label, framing device, or controlled accent color
- Avoid: random shapes, chaotic hierarchy, novelty navigation

## 7. Human Story

Best when people, relationships, service, transformation, or trust drive the decision.

- Composition: narrative sequences, portraits, quotes integrated with proof
- Type: approachable and conversational
- Imagery: real people, contextual moments, authentic environments
- Surfaces: supportive rather than dominant
- Rhythm: story, evidence, action
- Signature: names, voices, timelines, or before-and-after narratives
- Avoid: stock-photo sentiment, testimonial walls, unsupported claims

## 8. Soft Organic

Best for wellbeing, sustainability, family, nature, and approachable premium brands.

- Composition: flowing but aligned sections, gentle image transitions
- Type: soft display forms with clear functional typography
- Imagery: natural light, texture, calm environments
- Surfaces: tonal layers and selective curves
- Rhythm: calm, open, reassuring
- Signature: organic crop, tonal gradient, or nature-derived linework
- Avoid: excessive blobs, weak contrast, generic beige sameness

## 9. Structured Utility

Best for services where speed, clarity, comparison, or repeated use matters most.

- Composition: direct hierarchy, compact modules, visible pathways
- Type: functional, legible, moderately dense
- Imagery: selective and informative
- Surfaces: strong grouping, clear dividers, limited decoration
- Rhythm: efficient with focused moments of brand expression
- Signature: numbered pathway, compact comparison, or useful summary rail
- Avoid: generic SaaS dashboard styling, treating every action as a button

## 10. Experimental Editorial

Best for design-literate audiences and brands with permission to be distinctive.

- Composition: controlled asymmetry, unusual crops, editorial pacing
- Type: expressive hierarchy and intentional line breaks
- Imagery: art-directed, graphic, or culturally specific
- Surfaces: minimal containers; composition creates grouping
- Rhythm: contrast between quiet and expressive sections
- Signature: one memorable typographic or spatial device
- Avoid: unclear reading order, obscured CTAs, experimentation in form controls

## 11. Immersive Full-Screen Photography

Best when place, environment, architecture, travel, hospitality, property, or a highly visual product should create the immediate emotional impression.

- Composition: edge-to-edge photographic chapters with conversion content placed in controlled safe zones
- Type: concise display copy with highly legible supporting text
- Imagery: exceptional landscape-orientation photography with consistent grading and intentional focal areas
- Surfaces: minimal interface chrome; content sections may become quieter between immersive moments
- Rhythm: full-viewport opening, calmer information, then one or two additional full-bleed photographic chapters
- Signature: photography itself is the dominant spatial material rather than a framed content asset
- Avoid: framed hero images, large whitespace before the hero, carousels, autoplay video, uncontrolled text placement, weak or mismatched source imagery

### Required implementation rules

- Make the opening photographic composition at least `100svh` on desktop.
- Start the opening image at the top of the page. Do not place a separate whitespace or header section before it.
- Place navigation transparently over the hero or integrate it within the same full-viewport section.
- Extend the image edge-to-edge across the viewport. Do not add outer page margins around the opening image.
- Keep the value proposition and primary CTA completely visible within the first viewport at common laptop heights.
- Use a controlled overlay, gradient, focal-aware crop, or dedicated quiet image area to guarantee text contrast.
- Include at least one additional full-bleed photographic chapter when the available imagery supports it.
- On mobile, use at least `80svh`, preserve the subject through focal-aware cropping, and keep the primary CTA visible without requiring excessive scrolling.
- Load an appropriately sized responsive image and avoid unnecessary blocking media so the primary action remains usable quickly.
- If suitable imagery is unavailable, do not fake this direction with stretched or low-quality images; select a different direction.

### Conversion boundary

The photograph may dominate the viewport, but it must not dominate the decision path. Navigation, offer clarity, primary action, contrast, performance, and mobile usability remain mandatory.

## Controlled variation within a direction

Choose compatible values rather than a fixed template:

- Hero: full-bleed, offset, framed, typographic, image-collage
- Page width: contained, wide, mixed
- Density: calm, balanced, compact
- Image system: landscape-led, portrait-led, mixed editorial, technical
- Section transition: whitespace, rule, tonal shift, image bridge
- Evidence treatment: metrics, case studies, comparison, annotations
- Corner language: sharp, subtle, soft
- Accent behavior: sparse, structural, expressive

Keep one visual idea dominant. Do not select every expressive option simultaneously.

For **Immersive Full-Screen Photography**, the opening hero is not variable: it must follow that direction's required full-viewport implementation rules. Variation may occur in text placement, crop, overlay, and later section composition.
